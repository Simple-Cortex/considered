# Experience mode

Use when the work, world, performance, or authored moment should lead. The success signal is sustained attention and a distinct impression, not rapid task completion or dense information extraction.

The `EXP-` rules below are guidance rules: no linter emits them, and they surface only by reading this catalog at its routed stage.

## Frame

Name the intended encounter: where attention begins, what it discovers, and the moment that changes or releases it. State the work's source, ownership, and any motion, sound, or interaction constraints. A missing aesthetic preference is usually a nonblocking assumption. Ask only when rights, safety, accessibility, autoplay, audience restrictions, or delivery capability would materially change the experience.

## Structure

- `EXP-01` (the work holds the focal position): let P0 be the work or appointed moment, never a logo, navigation bar, or explanatory frame.
- Use P1 for the sequence, selected work, authored cue, or invitation that guides attention.
- Use P2 for quiet context, optional interpretation, and deliberate wayfinding.
- Put credits, extended process notes, alternate works, and deep controls at P3.
- Keep navigation, mute, captions, fullscreen, and utilities at P4 until agency makes them necessary.
- `EXP-02` (motion is authored, not decorative): give every transition a narrative or perceptual role. Stillness is a valid authored choice.
- `EXP-06` (no competing task on the surface): split a concurrent commerce, administration, or reference task into another surface rather than forcing a mode collision.

## Compose and harden

`EXP-03` (controls stay operable under immersion): controls must remain accessible without competing with the work. Provide keyboard operation, visible focus, captions or transcripts when applicable, reduced-motion behavior, and mute and pause control where media can play. `EXP-04` (fallback preserves the encounter): author a stable fallback for unsupported media or constrained devices.

`EXP-05` (point of view over spectacle): do not use spectacle as a substitute for point of view. Do not hide a safety, consent, or essential accessibility control in the name of immersion.

`EXP-07` (action color matches the emotional target): the surface's job sets the color temperature of its actions. On a calming or focus surface, a saturated red or orange primary reads as alarm — two independent blind reviews flagged exactly this on a breathing-session build. When the dealt direction's accent is alarm-coded, re-map it: keep the direction's character in structure and type, choose a calm-coded accent for the primary action, and reserve alarm hues for genuinely destructive or warning actions.

## Review

Check that interface chrome, explanation, and effects do not eclipse the work. Verify that motion is authored rather than decorative, controls appear when agency is useful, and fallback states preserve meaning. Name `Interface Eclipse` when the interface wins attention from the work.
