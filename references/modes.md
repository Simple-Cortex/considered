# Surface Modes

Purpose: Choose one human goal for the surface, then let that mode govern every structural and expressive decision.

Contents: mode contract, comparison, five mode playbooks, detection, mode purity, audit.
The `MODE-` rules below are guidance rules: no linter emits them, and they surface only by reading this catalog at its routed stage.

## Commit to one mode
`MODE-01` (mode declared before form): name the mode before choosing layout, components, tone, or motion.
State the human goal as a verb: believe, complete, understand, learn, or feel.
Make one outcome unmistakably easier than every neighboring outcome.
Let the mode set density, focal position, action posture, proof burden, and pacing.
Build for the human decision, not for the requested screen category.
Use the surface label only as evidence, since a homepage can operate and a dashboard can persuade.
Write the mode into the design contract so later passes preserve it.

`MODE-09` (the direction serves the genre): wherever user trust or an established genre governs a surface — signup and auth, payment and pricing, documentation and reference, daily-operate tools — genre conventions own the ground color, chrome, input affordances, and color semantics, and the dealt direction expresses itself in accents, type details, and structure. A terminal-styled signup form, an editorial docs re-theme, and a themed admin header are the same failure at three genres: the direction applied as costume where it reads as risk. `MODE-08`, `READ-07`, and `EXP-07` are instances of this rule; blind three-judge review has confirmed it independently on every genre tested. Adapting the dealt direction to the genre is required fidelity, not a re-roll.

`MODE-10` (the direction survives the register): the complement of `MODE-09`. When a house style or brand register arrives as a supplied fact, the register owns everything it names — but the dealt direction must still get at least one unmistakable, considered expression inside it: a signature structural move, a deliberate density rhythm, a scale contrast the register permits. Blind judges across two rounds called register-compliant builds with no such expression "generic" and "template-like" — register fidelity spent nowhere reads as the absence of design, not the presence of restraint. Find the degrees of freedom the register leaves open and spend the direction there; never by breaking the register, and never nowhere.

## Compact comparison

The table is the source for four rules: `MODE-03` (density matches frequency and expertise), `MODE-04` (action posture matches the mode), `MODE-05` (motion budget matches the mode), and `MODE-06` (the declared mode's characteristic failure is absent). Each mode playbook below restates its own row of them.

| Mode | Human goal | Success signal | Density | Focal position | Action posture | Motion budget | Characteristic failure |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Persuade | Decide to believe, trust, or commit | The visitor advances with informed confidence | Low to medium, paced | The sharpest claim with adjacent proof | One primary commitment, supporting paths recede | Medium, use motion to stage argument and evidence | Promise Parade: claims accumulate without proof or objection handling |
| Operate | Complete a task accurately and quickly | The task finishes with low effort and recoverable errors | Medium to high, frequency-adjusted | The current object, state, or next action | Direct, scoped, explicit, reversible where possible | Low, preserve orientation and confirm state change | Action Flatland: every control competes and scope disappears |
| Analyze | Build an explanation from evidence | The human can state what changed, why, and what follows | High where expertise supports it | The finding, anomaly, or comparison that changes the decision | Investigation first, action after interpretation | Low to medium, reveal change and causality | Chart Dump: available data replaces a ranked question |
| Read | Acquire and retain knowledge | The reader can find, comprehend, and recall the intended idea | Medium, rhythm-led | The current idea, proposition, or instructional step | Quiet, optional, placed at natural decision points | Low, support continuity and progress | Content Fog: presentation fragments the argument and weakens comprehension |
| Experience | Encounter a work, world, or performance on its own terms | Attention sustains and the work leaves a distinct impression | Variable, composition-led | The work itself or the moment the work appoints | Invitational, sparse, often delayed | High when authored, still purposeful | Interface Eclipse: chrome, explanation, or effects overpower the work |

## Persuade mode
### Commit
Win attention, then earn belief, then make commitment feel like the logical next step.
Choose one consequential promise and give it the strongest language and space.
Arrange the page as an argument, not as a component inventory.
Pair every meaningful claim with adjacent evidence.
Sequence proof against the visitor's actual doubts.
Make the primary commitment singular, visible, and honest.
### Build settings
**Human's goal:** Decide whether this offer deserves attention, trust, money, time, or reputation.
**Success signal:** Observe an informed next step, such as starting, buying, booking, contacting, or reviewing terms.
**Density expectation:** Keep density low to medium; use generous pacing for new claims and tighter density inside proof.
**Focal position:** Give the strongest relevant claim plus its proof the first visual victory.
**Action posture:** Present one primary commitment; make secondary paths useful but visibly subordinate.
**Motion budget:** Spend a medium budget on staging, demonstration, and transitions that increase belief.
**Characteristic failure:** Name Promise Parade when claims, features, and testimonials accumulate without a coherent case.
### Short brakes
Never substitute atmosphere for a specific promise.
Do not invent proof, customers, scarcity, or certainty.

## Operate mode
### Commit
Put the task, object, and current state in direct view.
Optimize repeated work for speed and rare work for guidance.
Rank actions by scope, consequence, and frequency.
Preserve context through every state change.
Choose defaults that safely complete the common case.
Make recovery part of the main path.
### Build settings
**Human's goal:** Complete a known task with acceptable effort and risk.
**Success signal:** Confirm correct completion, low time on task, few errors, and successful recovery.
**Density expectation:** Raise density with expertise and frequency; reduce chrome before reducing useful information.
**Focal position:** Give the active object, decisive status, or next required action the focal position.
**Action posture:** Use direct verbs, one primary per scope, and friction proportional to consequence.
**Motion budget:** Keep the budget low; use motion for continuity, causality, and completion feedback.
**Characteristic failure:** Name Action Flatland when equally styled controls erase priority and scope.

`MODE-08` (operate chrome stays quiet): daily-use operate surfaces keep navigation, headers, and section chrome near-monochrome and low-contrast; the direction's palette expresses itself in data color, status, and the primary action — never in filled navigation bars, emphasis rings around resting cards, or display-scale section headings. Shipped admin and operations tools converge on quiet chrome because a tool used hourly earns trust by disappearing; a themed header is persuade-mode chrome on an operate surface.
### Short brakes
Never make a destructive action the casual default.
Do not hide essential task state behind decorative presentation.

## Analyze mode
### Commit
Begin with the decision or question the evidence must support.
Lead with the finding that most changes interpretation.
Connect status, change, cause, and consequence in one readable chain.
Place comparisons beside the values they explain.
Use detail on demand to reward investigation without flattening the overview.
Turn each zone into an answer, not a data container.
### Build settings
**Human's goal:** Understand what happened, why it happened, and what the evidence permits them to conclude.
**Success signal:** Hear the human state a supported finding, identify uncertainty, and choose a sound next inquiry or action.
**Density expectation:** Use medium to high density, calibrated to expertise, cadence, and analytical depth.
**Focal position:** Give the decisive finding, anomaly, or comparison the strongest encoding.
**Action posture:** Support filtering, comparison, annotation, and drill first; offer operational action after meaning is established.
**Motion budget:** Use a low to medium budget for transitions across time, filters, and causal relationships.
**Characteristic failure:** Name Chart Dump when data availability determines the surface and no question governs inclusion.
### Short brakes
Never present a context-free number as an insight.
Do not let chart type become the grouping principle.

## Read mode
### Commit
Shape a clear line of thought from entry to conclusion.
Give headings real argumentative work.
Use typography, measure, and spacing to establish a durable reading rhythm.
Place definitions, examples, and references where comprehension needs them.
Support scanning without sacrificing sequential meaning.
Let navigation answer where am I, what comes next, and how do I return.
### Build settings
**Human's goal:** Find, comprehend, retain, or apply knowledge.
**Success signal:** Observe successful retrieval, sustained reading, accurate recall, or correct application.
**Density expectation:** Use medium density with stable measure and clear rhythm; vary density around examples and reference material.
**Focal position:** Give the current idea, proposition, or instructional step priority over surrounding chrome.
**Action posture:** Keep actions quiet and contextual, such as continue, copy, save, cite, or try.
**Motion budget:** Keep the budget low; use motion to preserve location and reveal optional explanation.
**Characteristic failure:** Name Content Fog when excessive cards, decoration, or fragmentation breaks the line of thought.
### Short brakes
Never turn every paragraph into a module.
Do not make navigation louder than the knowledge.

## Experience mode
### Commit
Let the work establish its own pace, scale, and sequence.
Choose a point of view and carry it fully through composition, sound, interaction, and transition.
Create anticipation, contrast, and release as authored moments.
Make controls feel native to the world of the work.
Use the viewport as a stage rather than a container grid.
Give stillness as much intention as motion.
### Build settings
**Human's goal:** Encounter, explore, or feel the work rather than merely extract information from it.
**Success signal:** Sustain attention, invite exploration, and leave a distinct, attributable impression.
**Density expectation:** Let composition set density; move between emptiness and intensity with purpose.
**Focal position:** Give the work itself, or the moment it appoints, visual command.
**Action posture:** Invite rather than instruct; reveal controls when agency becomes useful.
**Motion budget:** Allow a high budget when motion is authored into the experience; make timing and restraint part of the direction.
**Characteristic failure:** Name Interface Eclipse when controls, explanatory copy, or effects compete with the work.
### Short brakes
Never use spectacle as a substitute for a point of view.
Do not interrupt the authored sequence with competing conversion furniture.

## Detect the mode from an undeclared brief
`MODE-07` (mode detected from the human verb) governs this section.
Extract the human, the verb, the consequence, the frequency, and the observable finish.
Ask what must be different in the human after using the surface.
Map **believe or commit** to persuade.
Map **complete or change** to operate.
Map **explain or compare** to analyze.
Map **learn or retrieve** to read.
Map **encounter or feel** to experience.
Use the strongest consequential verb, not the artifact noun.
Inspect requested content as supporting evidence, not as a mode declaration.
Choose the mode that owns the final decision when several verbs appear.
State one assumption when the brief leaves the human or outcome unclear.
Request clarification when two outcomes carry equal consequence.

## Enforce mode purity
`MODE-02` (mode purity): treat mixing two modes on one surface as a defect.
Choose a dominant mode and move the other goal to a separate surface, step, or clearly bounded region.
Let a secondary region support the dominant mode without adopting its own focal claim or primary action.
Test purity by asking whether one success signal governs the whole surface.
Split the surface when two success signals demand different density, pacing, focal position, or action posture.
Name the defect Mode Collision when neither goal can win without weakening the other.

## Audit
Confirm that the first focal element advances the declared human goal.
Confirm that density reflects frequency, expertise, and consequence.
Confirm that the primary action matches the mode's action posture.
Confirm that motion spends attention only where the mode earns it.
Confirm that the named characteristic failure is absent.
Confirm that one success signal governs the full surface.
